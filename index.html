MTN Ghana: ready to share data, not to run the tax tool

One completed questionnaire from Ghana's largest mobile-money operator. It is a single institutional response, so it describes a stated position rather than a measured share. Read against the worker survey, three tensions stand out.

Data, yes. Tool, no.

MTN would share mobile-money transaction data with GRA under legal frameworks, but says an AI tax dashboard for workers is outside its scope. GRA has to own the taxpayer-facing tool.

Automatic deduction meets distrust

MTN's preferred optimisation is automatic tax deduction from MoMo transactions. Among workers, —% distrust government and —% fear misuse of their data. Silent deduction risks pushing activity back into cash.

Both name the same barrier

MTN expects workers to resist digital platforms for fear of taxation, and names transaction fees as the main reason they avoid MoMo. That matches the workers' own “taxes too high” answer at —%.

From the Ghana evidence to an African policy roadmap

The paper's recommendations, sequenced and tied to the survey result that justifies each one. The order follows the data: trust and literacy come before automation, because acceptance of AI collapses where awareness is missing.

Phase 1 · Year 1 · Build trust

Make taxes visible and teach the tools

  • Publish where informal-sector taxes go, market by market. Evidence: —% of those willing to pay doubt it makes a difference.
  • Digital tax literacy through trade associations, MoMo agents, radio and WhatsApp. Evidence: awareness lifts positive views from —% to —%.
  • A data-protection charter for tax use of MoMo data, agreed with operators. Evidence: privacy concern at —% of workers and — of — professionals.
Phase 2 · Years 2–3 · Pilot

Voluntary digital filing with the ready segment

  • Government-endorsed e-invoicing app piloted in Accra, Kumasi and Tamale markets and transport terminals. Evidence: professionals rate electronic invoicing the most effective technology.
  • Register businesses through associations (GPRTU, hairdressers, artisans), with group incentives. Evidence: record-keeping is the top challenge for — of — professionals.
  • Target the —% ready adopters first; keep an assisted cash channel for the resistant —%.
Phase 3 · Years 4–5 · Scale with oversight

AI-assisted assessment under audit

  • Ghana Card–TIN–MoMo linkage for risk scoring and presumptive assessment, using aggregated data MTN has said it can share.
  • An AI ethics committee auditing models for bias; human review of every flagged case. Evidence: the most common worker fear is AI used to cheat (— of — comments).
  • Track success as a broader base before revenue: — of — professionals expect that outcome first.

Method and limits

How the data was built

  • — informal-sector questionnaires (four scanned pages each) coded by hand into a respondent-level file; ticks and circles both counted.
  • Shares use those who answered each question as the base; blanks are excluded, never counted as “no”.
  • 95% Wilson intervals for every informal-sector share; Spearman, Kruskal–Wallis and Fisher exact tests for associations.
  • Professional results transcribed from the Google Forms summary (n = —); MTN from one scanned questionnaire.

What the data cannot show

  • A convenience sample of this size is not representative of Ghana's informal sector, let alone Sub-Saharan Africa's; it is case-study evidence, and shares carry wide intervals.
  • Answers are self-reported and may lean towards what respondents think is expected, especially on tax.
  • Associations are not causes: education, awareness and attitudes move together.
  • The readiness score is a transparent heuristic, not a validated scale.
  • One respondent's last page was missing from the scan; that form has no AI answers.

Coding notes

  • Income bands are monthly, in Ghana cedis.
  • The cash-preference question was answered by fewer workers than hesitated about MoMo, including some who said they were comfortable with MoMo.
  • Comment themes are single-coded by the analyst; the full text of every comment is on the Workers' voices page.
